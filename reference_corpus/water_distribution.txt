Water distribution systems routinely lose a substantial share of treated water to leaks, bursts and measurement error. Utilities classify losses into real losses, meaning physical escapes from the pipe network, and apparent losses, meaning under-registration by faulty meters and unauthorised consumption. Acoustic leak detectors, pressure loggers and district metered areas are the standard toolkit for locating real losses.

Repair economics favour targeting clusters: a small number of failing pipe sections typically accounts for the majority of lost volume, so instrumented surveys that identify high-loss zones deliver rapid payback at current bulk-water prices. Meter under-registration is a quieter but persistent source of apparent loss; periodic testing of commercial meters frequently recovers several percent of billed revenue because aged meters under-record low and medium flows.

Demand-side measures complement network repairs. Smart metering gives customers consumption feedback and enables leak alerts at the property level, while greywater recycling and rainwater harvesting reduce potable demand in new construction. Retrospective retrofits of recycling systems in existing buildings are usually costly, and enforcement of harvesting mandates strains inspection capacity, so programme designers commonly sequence inexpensive, high-yield interventions first and treat behavioural measures as longer-term options.
