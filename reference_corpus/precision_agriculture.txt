Precision agriculture is a farming management concept that uses observation, measurement and targeted response to optimise crop production while reducing input use. Data sources include satellite imagery, soil sensors, weather stations, machine telematics and yield monitors mounted on harvesting equipment. Analysis of these inputs produces zone-specific recommendations for fertiliser application, irrigation, seeding density and pest control.

Documented benefits include reductions in fertiliser and water use of roughly ten to twenty percent, improved yield stability, and traceability records that attract quality premiums from buyers. The economics depend on farm scale and data infrastructure: sensor grids and subscription analytics services favour larger operations, while cooperative data-sharing arrangements allow smaller growers to pool costs and benchmark results.

Barriers to adoption are well studied. Interoperability between vendor platforms remains weak, so data captured by one machine may not transfer to another vendor's software. Connectivity gaps in rural regions limit real-time services, and model outputs trained on other regions may transfer poorly to local soil and climate conditions. Effective programmes therefore pair technology with agronomist support, treat model outputs as decision support rather than prescriptive instructions, and stage investment across seasons to match cash flow.
